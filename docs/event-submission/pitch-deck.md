# Riskora — six-slide pitch deck source

This is the editable content for [Riskora-pitch-deck.pptx](Riskora-pitch-deck.pptx). The deck describes the present prototype and labels hypotheses. The slide 3 illustration is a flow schematic; it is not a captured product screenshot.

The PowerPoint file is generated by `python scripts/build_pitch_deck.py` from the repository root after `python -m pip install python-pptx`. Team: Ansil Muhammed N S and Muhammed Ihsan K I.

## 1. Problem and user

**Fund disclosures show yesterday's portfolio. Researchers still need a disciplined way to ask what may appear next.**

- Intended user: an analyst or small research team studying Indian equity mutual-fund disclosures.
- A disclosed holding is a dated position, not a record of a purchase order.
- Comparing statements manually can confuse new additions with existing holdings, missing data, or late publication.

## 2. Insight and why now

**The useful unit is a comparable pair of published snapshots, with a strict information cutoff.**

- A new addition needs a complete earlier statement and a later comparable statement.
- A candidate and its signals must be knowable at the earlier cutoff.
- Public disclosure routes exist, but this prototype currently requires user-normalized imports; no automated historical source has been verified.
- Differentiation hypothesis: a traceable ranking and explicit unknown cases are more useful than an opaque prediction label. This has not been tested with customers.

## 3. Solution demonstration

**A four-step research loop.**

1. Load the labelled synthetic sample or import attributed CSV statements.
2. Inspect dated holdings in Fund explorer.
3. Train and inspect later-period evaluation, including simple baselines.
4. Open candidate rankings and supporting signals for the selected fund.

Show this sequence live using the [three-minute runbook](demo-runbook.md). The current deck contains an accurate schematic of this flow, not product screenshots.

## 4. Technical approach

**Source files → validated SQLite records → point-in-time labels/features → ranking and evidence.**

- FastAPI, SQLite, React/Vite; scikit-learn logistic classifier.
- User CSV imports are separated from fictional synthetic records.
- Expanding-window validation and a final holdout compare the model with frequency and sector/industry baselines.
- Optional language-model extraction is constrained to verbatim source excerpts; it is unconfigured by default.

## 5. Value and go-to-market hypothesis

**Potential value: less manual sorting and a better audit trail for research teams.**

- Initial users to interview: fund research analysts and independent research desks.
- Potential buyer and pricing hypothesis: a team license for research workflows, subject to discovery.
- Proposed first channel: supervised pilots with teams already reviewing disclosures.
- No interviews, paying customers, conversion, or market-size evidence are claimed here.

## 6. Next steps and risks

**Prove data quality and usefulness before commercial claims.**

- Obtain and validate permissioned historical disclosure and market data.
- Test ranking quality across funds and periods; audit corporate actions, survivorship, missing data, and calibration.
- Interview users and test whether an auditable shortlist improves their workflow.
- Make a hosted demo or recording, and replace the schematic with actual screenshots.
- Keep ranking scores separate from trade claims and investment advice.

The present synthetic evaluation demonstrates the pipeline only. It does not establish predictive accuracy on Indian markets.
